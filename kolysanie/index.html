The Daily Pause

Rocking to sleep

Lie down with your phone on your belly. It feels you breathe, and soft waves of sound rock you, wave by wave, toward a slower rhythm.

  1. Lie on your back in bed, with a pillow under your head.
  2. Rest the phone flat on your belly, just above your navel, screen up. Let it lie there, no need to hold it.
  3. Close your eyes and listen. A soft voice guides you at first, then only the waves. Breathe in as they rise, out as they fall.

On iPhone, if you hear nothing, check that the silent switch is off and the volume is up.

Turn the volume down low before you begin. The screen stays on, but sinks almost to black. Your phone's motion sensor is read only here, on your phone. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent. A gentle way to wind down, not a medical treatment.

StartEnd

Why it works. When you breathe slowly, around six breaths a minute, your heart rate rises and falls more with each breath. Across more than two hundred studies, slow breathing raised this sign of the body's calming, resting side.1 In a small study of people with insomnia, twenty minutes of breathing at six breaths a minute before bed helped them fall asleep sooner and wake less in the night.2 Here the waves start at your own pace and slow down only as far as you follow them.

  1. Laborde S et al. Effects of voluntary slow breathing on heart rate and heart rate variability: a systematic review and a meta-analysis. Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2022.
  2. Tsai HJ et al. Efficacy of paced breathing for insomnia: enhances vagal activity and improves sleep quality. Psychophysiology, 2015.

A gentle way to wind down, not a medical treatment. If sleep is hard for weeks, or you feel dizzy when breathing slowly, breathe normally and talk to a doctor.
